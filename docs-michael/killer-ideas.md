## In more detail

**1 · Sport Form Coach.** You film yourself playing (say, table tennis) from the side and hand the clip to the app. It samples frames, reads your stance, footwork, recovery, and stroke types, and returns a report: strengths, the top things to fix, drills, and one focus for next time. It is honest that it cannot measure spin or exact ball speed.

**2 · Video Clipper.** You give it a long recording — a podcast, a webinar, a match, a lecture. It finds the strongest moments and returns several short vertical clips with captions and titles, plus thumbnail options, ready to post.

**3 · Design Critic.** You upload a screenshot of an app or website. It returns the same image with annotations (markers/labels) pointing to specific problems, plus a written list of concrete fixes: spacing, contrast, hierarchy, wording.

**4 · Screenshot-to-Guide.** You provide a series of screenshots of a process (or a screen recording). It assembles them into a written step-by-step how-to, placing the right image at each step — usable as documentation, onboarding, or a support article.

**5 · Form Filler.** You photograph a blank paper form or a PDF. It reads the fields, fills them from information you give it (or that it already knows), returns a completed PDF, and explains any field it was unsure about instead of guessing.

**6 · Damage Assessor.** You photograph damage — a dented car, a water-stained wall, a cracked phone. It describes the damage, lists likely repairs and parts, gives a rough cost range, and drafts an insurance claim or repair request.

**7 · Classroom Assistant.** You photograph a student's handwritten worksheet or homework. It marks it, writes specific feedback, highlights the mistakes, and can produce a short lesson or diagram for the concept they missed.

**8 · Seller Assistant.** You photograph products. It writes the listing (title, description, tags) and ad copy, and produces imagery or creatives for the listing or a campaign.

**9 · Sales Call Coach.** You upload a recorded sales call. It transcribes it and returns timestamped coaching — where the rep talked too much, missed discovery questions, or fumbled objections — plus an overall score and the single biggest improvement.

**10 · Lecture-to-Notes.** You record a lecture or talk (audio or video). It returns structured notes, a short quiz to test yourself, and an audio recap you can listen to later.

**11 · Text-based Video Editor.** Instead of dragging clips on a timeline, you describe the edit in a sentence — "cut the first 20 seconds, remove long pauses, add captions, make a 30-second version" — and it performs the edit and hands back the new video.

**12 · Document-to-Podcast.** You give it a PDF — a report, a paper, a policy. It rewrites it as a spoken script and renders it as an audio episode, optionally in your own voice.

**13 · Talking Avatar Reply.** You give it a message or a task. It returns a short video of an avatar speaking the reply, lip-synced — a personal-feeling answer you can forward.

**14 · Camera Companion.** A person who is blind or has low vision points their phone camera; the app describes what is in view and narrates it by voice — obstacles, signs, text, products, scenes — in real time.

**15 · Live Meeting Copilot.** During a live call it listens quietly, surfaces relevant facts, flags missed questions or risks, and afterwards produces notes and a drafted follow-up.

**Also killed-it:** **Hiring Screener** — upload recorded video interviews and get a ranked shortlist scored against the role, with the evidence for each score. **Real-time Voice Translator / Dubbing** — speak and hear the translation in your own voice, or dub a video into another language.
