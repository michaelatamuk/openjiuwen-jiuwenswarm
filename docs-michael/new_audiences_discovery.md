Good reframe. For each audience, the question is: where do they look for new tools, who do they trust, and what format of content actually reaches them.

---

### Business professionals (non-developers)
They don't browse GitHub. They discover tools through **LinkedIn posts from peers**, **ProductHunt launches**, **newsletter sponsorships** (e.g. TLDR, The Hustle), and **one viral demo video** that a colleague forwards. The trigger is usually "I saw someone use this to do X in 2 minutes." A short screen recording showing a swarm completing a real business task — competitive research, summarizing a market — posted on LinkedIn and Twitter by a few influential accounts is the highest-leverage move. No feature announcement, just a concrete before/after.

---

### Academic researchers (scientists, not AI researchers)
They discover through **papers that cite the tool**, **conference workshops**, and **being tagged in a relevant GitHub repo**. A domain scientist won't search for "multi-agent framework" — but they will read a paper titled "Automating Systematic Literature Review with Multi-Agent Orchestration" that uses JiuwenSwarm as its infrastructure. The discovery mechanism is **publish one real paper in a domain journal** (not an AI venue) co-authored with a lab that used JiuwenSwarm for their actual research. That single paper becomes the entry point for everyone in that discipline who faces the same workflow.

---

### Data scientists
They live on **Kaggle**, **Hugging Face**, **Towards Data Science**, and **Reddit r/MachineLearning**. A working notebook on Kaggle or a HuggingFace Space that uses JiuwenSwarm to solve a real dataset problem — not a toy demo — gets shared naturally. HuggingFace in particular: a Space with a JiuwenSwarm-powered pipeline gets visibility from HF's own promotion of interesting Spaces. Getting on **HN front page** once reaches the entire data science community in a day.

---

### DevOps / SRE teams
They find tools through **Hacker News**, **r/devops**, **DevOps Days talks**, and **posts on the incident.io or PagerDuty community blogs**. The highest-reach move is a well-written HN post titled "Show HN: We built an incident response swarm that triages alerts automatically" — SRE communities respond strongly to real operational war stories. A write-up of one real production incident where JiuwenSwarm helped, posted on a personal engineering blog and submitted to HN, reaches this entire audience.

---

### Content creators and media teams
They discover tools through **Twitter/X**, **YouTube tutorials**, and **what other creators talk about publicly**. The discovery mechanism here is getting **one YouTuber or newsletter writer with a large following to actually use it and show the output**. This audience doesn't read documentation — they watch someone else do it first. Reaching 2-3 creators in the "AI tools" niche (e.g. Matt Wolfe, similar) with a working demo tailored to their use case is the entire strategy.

---

### Educators and students
Teachers discover ed-tech through **professional learning communities**, **conference demos** (ISTE, EdTechX), and increasingly through **GitHub Education partnerships**. Students discover through their professors and through **GitHub trending**. Getting listed in GitHub Education's toolbox, or having one professor assign JiuwenSwarm in a university AI course, creates a semester-long cohort of users who talk about it publicly afterward.

---

### AI / ML researchers
They discover through **arXiv**, **Papers With Code**, and **GitHub stars from people they follow**. The move is straightforward: publish a systems paper, list JiuwenSwarm in Papers With Code with reproducible benchmarks, make sure the GitHub repo has a strong README with comparison numbers. Researchers follow what gets starred by other researchers — one endorsement from a known lab's Twitter account reaches thousands of researchers immediately.

---

### Agency owners and freelancers
They discover through **Indie Hackers**, **ProductHunt**, **Twitter/X bootstrapper community**, and **podcasts** like My First Million or Indie Hackers podcast. The discovery trigger for this group is almost always a story: "here's how I delivered a client project in half the time using agent swarms." A case study written by a freelancer who actually used JiuwenSwarm, posted on Indie Hackers, gets more traction with this audience than any feature announcement.

---

### Common thread
For almost every audience the highest-ROI move is **one piece of authentic content showing a real use case in their context**, distributed through a channel they already trust — not a general product page or feature list. The IDE plugin worked because it appeared inside the developers' own tool. For each audience the equivalent is: appear in the format and channel they already inhabit.
